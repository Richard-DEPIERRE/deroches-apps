The mileage rate for an electric car depends on whose car it is.

- **Your own electric car**: there is no separate electric rate. HMRC's approved mileage rates cover all "cars and
  vans (including electric/hybrid cars and vans)": **55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the 2026 to 2027
  tax year** (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027; it was 45p before 6 April 2026), then **25p a mile**. An employer can pay up
  to the resulting approved amount (business miles × the rate) each tax year without reporting it to HMRC or taking
  tax off it.
- **A company electric car**: HMRC's **advisory electric rate**. From 1 December 2026 it is **7p a mile for home
  charging and 15p a mile for public charging**, the same as from 1 September 2026. In gov.uk's words, if the rate an
  employer pays for business miles "is no higher than the advisory fuel rates", "there will be no taxable profit and no
  Class 1A National Insurance to pay". HMRC reviews the rate every quarter (the next review is due on 1 March 2027), so
  check gov.uk's "Advisory fuel rates" page for the latest.

The two rates, an example of each, hybrids, and the records to keep are below.

## Your own electric car: the approved mileage rates

When you use your own car for work and your employer pays you per mile (a "mileage allowance payment"), the most it can
pay without tax is the **approved amount**: your business miles in the tax year × the approved rate. The claim itself,
journey by journey, is in [how a mileage claim to your employer works](/en/guides/mileage-claim/). The rate is the
same for an electric car as for a petrol or diesel one:

| Car or van (electric, hybrid, petrol or diesel) | 2026 to 2027 tax year (from 6 April 2026) | Up to 2025 to 2026 |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------: | -----------------: |
| First 10,000 business miles in the tax year | 55p a mile | 45p a mile |
| Each business mile over 10,000 | 25p a mile | 25p a mile |

- **The 10,000 miles count per tax year**, all your cars and vans together, in that job and in any "associated
  employment" as section 230 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 defines it.
- **Charging is included.** gov.uk says the approved rate "covers the cost of owning and running your vehicle", and
  that fuel, electricity, vehicle tax, MOTs and repairs can't be claimed separately.
- **Above the approved amount**, the employer reports the excess on a P11D or adds it to your pay and takes tax off.
- **National Insurance** uses 55p for every business mile by car or van from 6 April 2026 (45p before), with no
  10,000-mile band.

The 55p is in section 230 of the Act, for the 2026 to 2027 tax year and later years, and on HMRC's rates pages, checked
on 18 November 2026. For the 2027 to 2028 tax year, check HMRC's "Travel — mileage and fuel rates and allowances" page.
The rates for motorcycles, bicycles and passengers are in [HMRC mileage rates for every vehicle and case](/en/guides/hmrc-mileage-rates/).

### Example: your own electric car, 2026 to 2027

A fictional example of HMRC's method. An employee drives their own electric car 3,200 business miles between 6 April
2026 and 5 April 2027.

| Step | Amount |
| ---- | -----: |
| 3,200 business miles × 55p (all within the first 10,000) | £1,760 |
| **Approved amount for the tax year** | **£1,760** |

Their employer can pay them up to £1,760 for those miles without tax. Anything it pays above £1,760 is taxable.

### If your employer pays less

gov.uk says that when an employer pays less than the approved amount, the employee "will be able to get tax relief
(called Mileage Allowance Relief, or MAR) on the unused balance". The guide to
[mileage allowance relief](/en/guides/mileage-allowance-relief/) explains how it is worked out and claimed.

## A company electric car: the advisory electric rate

The advisory rates are for company cars only: gov.uk says "these rates only apply to employees using a company car".
An employer uses them to:

- **reimburse business travel** in a company car, or
- work out what an employee **repays for fuel used for private travel**.

For fully electric company cars, the rates are:

| Charging location | From 1 December 2026 | 1 September to 30 November 2026 |
| ----------------- | -------------------: | ------------------------------: |
| Home charger | 7p a mile | 7p a mile |
| Public charger | 15p a mile | 15p a mile |

What gov.uk and HMRC's employer guide add:

- **No obligation.** HMRC's guide 480 says "there's no obligation to use the advisory fuel rates". gov.uk adds that an
  employer whose costs differ "can use your own rates to reflect your situation".
- **Paying more.** A higher rate can be used "as long as you can show that the fuel cost per mile is higher", for
  example a public charger that costs more per mile than the public rate. Paid above the advisory rate without showing
  that, the excess is taxable and counts as earnings for Class 1 National Insurance.
- **Home and public charging both.** "You can apportion the mileage based on how much charging happens at each place.
  The apportionment calculation should be fair and reasonable."
- **Electricity and the fuel benefit.** HMRC's employer rates page notes: "Electricity is not a fuel for car fuel
  benefit purposes."

### How the rate is set, and when it changes

HMRC reviews the advisory rates **every quarter, on 1 March, 1 June, 1 September and 1 December**. The home rate is
worked out from the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero's yearly domestic electricity price, updated each
quarter with the electricity part of the Office for National Statistics' Consumer Prices Index; the public rate from
the Zapmap price index for slow and fast public chargers (under 50 kW).
gov.uk says the previous rates can be used for up to 1 month from the date new rates apply.

The figures in this guide are the rates from 1 December 2026, which HMRC published on 18 November 2026, read on
28 November 2026. HMRC's next quarterly review is due on 1 March 2027: check gov.uk's "Advisory fuel rates" page for
the rate in force on the day you need it.

### Example: a company electric car, December 2026

A fictional example, at the rates from 1 December 2026. An employee drives their company electric car 400 business
miles in December 2026.

| How the car was charged | Working | Amount |
| ----------------------- | ------- | -----: |
| All at home | 400 × 7p | £28 |
| All at public chargers | 400 × 15p | £60 |
| About three-quarters at home, a quarter public, miles split the same way | 300 × 7p + 100 × 15p | £36 |

Paid at or below these figures for business miles, gov.uk says there is no taxable profit and no Class 1A National
Insurance to pay.

## Hybrids

For company cars, gov.uk says "hybrid cars are treated as either petrol or diesel cars for advisory fuel rates": they
use the petrol or diesel advisory fuel rate for their engine size, not the electric rate. In your own car, a hybrid uses
the same approved mileage rates as any car (55p and 25p for 2026 to 2027).

## Paying for charging yourself in a company car

gov.uk's guide to job expenses says: "You can claim tax relief on the money you've spent on fuel and electricity, for
business trips in your company car. Keep records to show the actual cost of the fuel. If your employer reimburses some
of the money, you can claim relief on the difference." That relief rests on the actual cost you paid, not on the
advisory rate.

## The records to keep

- **Your own car.** gov.uk asks you to keep "records of the dates and mileage of your work journeys". A claim to HMRC
  goes in with copies of your mileage logs, which must show the reason for every journey and the postcodes where it
  started and ended. Travel to and from your usual work doesn't count, "unless it's a temporary place of work".
- **A company car.** When an employee repays private fuel, gov.uk says there is no fuel benefit charge if all private
  travel mileage is correctly recorded and the correct rate (or higher) is used. If your employer splits payments between home and public charging, ask it what
  it needs to do that.

A log line per business trip covers both: the date, where from and where to (with postcodes), the purpose and the
business miles.

For your own car, Roulekm (coming soon to iPhone and Android) keeps each business trip with its date, start, end,
purpose and miles, and totals them over the UK tax year at HMRC's approved rates. It doesn't apply the advisory
electric rate, so it doesn't work out company-car payments.

## What to check for your own case

This guide explains HMRC's rates and method. It doesn't say what you are owed. A few points depend on your situation:

- **Whose car it is, and what your employer pays.** Your employer's expenses policy or payroll team can tell you
  whether the car counts as yours or a company car, and the rate per mile it pays.
- **Which journeys are business travel.** For a journey of your own, ask HMRC or a tax adviser.
- **The rate in force on the day.** The advisory electric rate changes each quarter: check gov.uk's "Advisory fuel
  rates" page.
- **Company car tax.** The tax on having a company car is a separate rule: HMRC's rates page says it is based on the
  car's list price and accessories times a percentage set by its CO2 emissions and fuel. Your employer or HMRC can tell
  you your figure.

HMRC's guidance or a tax adviser.*
