If you're a sole trader, or a partner in a partnership that has no company as a partner, you can claim the cost of
driving your own car, van or motorcycle for your business in one of two ways: a **flat rate per business mile**, which
HMRC calls simplified expenses, or the **actual costs** of running the vehicle, with capital allowances for buying it.
For cars and vans, the flat rate is **55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the 2026 to 2027 tax year**
(45p before 6 April 2026), then 25p a mile; motorcycles are 24p a mile. The amount goes in your business expenses on the
self-employment pages of your Self Assessment return. For the 2025 to 2026 tax year, the online return is due by
**31 January 2027**.

Employed, or a director of your own company? gov.uk says that if you run a limited company you are not self-employed,
even if you're its owner and only employee, and limited companies can't use simplified expenses. Employees who use their
own vehicle for work are covered by [mileage allowance relief](/en/guides/mileage-allowance-relief/), in a separate
guide.

## The two ways to claim

### A flat rate per business mile (simplified expenses)

You multiply your business miles by HMRC's rate instead of adding up what the vehicle cost you. gov.uk lists the
vehicles it can be used for:

- cars, except those designed for commercial use, "for example, black cabs, hackney carriages or dual control driving
  instructors' cars";
- goods vehicles, for example vans;
- motorcycles.

HMRC's Business Income Manual (BIM75005) says the rate covers buying, running and maintaining the vehicle: fuel, oil,
servicing, repairs, insurance, vehicle tax and MOT, and its depreciation too. **Parking, tolls and congestion charges**
on a business journey aren't in the rate: they're claimed on top, like other travel such as train fares. Because only
business miles are counted, HMRC's notes to the full self-employment pages say there's no adjustment to make for
private use of a vehicle on the flat rate.

### The actual costs, with capital allowances

The other way is to claim the business part of what you actually spend. gov.uk's list of car, van and travel expenses
includes vehicle insurance, repairs and servicing, fuel, parking, hire charges, vehicle tax, licence fees and breakdown
cover. If you also use the vehicle privately, you claim only the business part.

The cost of the vehicle itself is not a running cost. With traditional accounting, it's claimed as **capital
allowances**. With cash basis accounting, gov.uk says a car is claimed as a capital allowance (as long as you're not
using simplified expenses for it), and other types of vehicle as allowable expenses. The SA103 notes add that capital
allowances are reduced by the private use proportion too.

### The rules on choosing, as gov.uk words them

- "You cannot claim simplified expenses for a vehicle you've already claimed capital allowances for, or you've included
  as an expense when you worked out your business profits."
- "You do not have to use flat rates for all your vehicles."
- "Once you use the flat rates for a vehicle, you must continue to do so as long as you use that vehicle for your
  business."

HMRC's manual puts the last point this way: "The business can only change to or from an 'actual' basis when a vehicle
is replaced." The law is in sections 94D to 94F of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005.

One more rule: if you use the £1,000 tax-free trading allowance, you can't claim any expenses at all, mileage included.

gov.uk has a simplified expenses checker that compares what you could claim each way.

## HMRC's flat rates per business mile

| Vehicle | 2026 to 2027 tax year (from 6 April 2026) | Before 6 April 2026 (up to 2025 to 2026) |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: |
| Car or goods vehicle, first 10,000 business miles | 55p a mile | 45p a mile |
| Car or goods vehicle, each business mile over 10,000 | 25p a mile | 25p a mile |
| Motorcycle | 24p a mile | 24p a mile |

- The 55p comes from section 2 of the Taxation (Energy and Vehicles) Act 2026, which applies it to the 2026 to 2027 tax
  year and later years.
- **The 10,000 miles are shared.** Section 94F of the 2005 Act counts the business miles of all the cars and goods
  vehicles your business uses the flat rate for together. The number of people in the vehicle doesn't change the rate.
- **There is no flat rate for bicycles.** Simplified expenses cover cars, goods vehicles and motorcycles only. The 20p a
  mile for bicycles that you may have seen is HMRC's approved rate for employees. gov.uk lists bicycles among the
  business vehicles you can claim capital allowances on with traditional accounting.
- **If your accounts don't run from 6 April to 5 April**, HMRC's manual (BIM75005) says each tax year's profit is
  worked out with that tax year's mileage rates, with examples for accounts running to 31 December 2026.

These are the rates on gov.uk and in the Act, checked on 17 November 2026. For the 2027 to 2028 tax year, check gov.uk's
simplified expenses page.

The rates for employees, bicycles and passengers sit next to these in
[HMRC mileage rates for every vehicle and case](/en/guides/hmrc-mileage-rates/).

## Worked example: the 2025 to 2026 tax year

A fictional example, to show HMRC's method. A sole trader whose accounts run from 6 April to 5 April uses the flat rate
for their car. They drove 11,500 business miles between 6 April 2025 and 5 April 2026, the tax year of the return due
by 31 January 2027.

| Step | Amount |
| ---- | -----: |
| First 10,000 business miles × 45p | £4,500 |
| Next 1,500 business miles × 25p | £375 |
| **Vehicle expenses at the flat rate** | **£4,875** |

Business parking they paid during the year would be added on top. The same 11,500 miles in the 2026 to 2027 tax year
would come to £5,875 (10,000 × 55p, plus 1,500 × 25p).

This is an expense, not a refund: it comes off the business's profit before Income Tax is worked out. gov.uk's own
example: £40,000 of turnover and £10,000 of allowable expenses means Income Tax is paid on the remaining £30,000.

## What counts as a business journey, and what doesn't

The Act counts a journey, or an identifiable part of one, "made wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the trade".
HMRC's manual says the flat rate "is not available for private journeys, such as travel from home to work, or for
journeys that serve both a business and a private purpose", and gov.uk says you can't claim for "travel between home and
work".

- **Not a business journey:** a florist drives from home to their own shop each morning. HMRC's manual (BIM37605) says a
  shopkeeper's travel between home and the shop is not allowable, even if they do the office work at home. Stopping at a
  supplier on the way doesn't change that (BIM37630).
- **A business journey:** the same florist drives from the shop to deliver an order to a customer. The manual quotes the
  courts on a barrister: once at their chambers, travel on to the courts is "wholly and exclusively" for the
  profession.

Some trades have no fixed place of business. For a trader who works at a series of temporary sites and is based at home
("a typical example would be a jobbing builder"), HMRC's manual (BIM37620) allows travel between home and the sites.

## Where it goes on your return

For the 2025 to 2026 tax year, the self-employment pages of the return are:

- **SA103S (short)**, if your turnover was under £90,000: box 12, "Car, van and travel expenses – after private use
  proportion". With turnover under £90,000 you may put your total expenses in box 20 instead of filling in each box.
- **SA103F (full)**, for turnover of £90,000 or more, among other cases: box 20, "Car, van and travel expenses". Below
  £90,000 the notes let most people put a single total in box 31.

gov.uk says to include the flat-rate amounts "in the total for your expenses in your Self Assessment tax return".
Capital allowances go in their own boxes, not in expenses. In a partnership, gov.uk says the nominated partner manages
the partnership's tax returns and keeps the business records, and each partner also sends their own return.

**Deadline:** for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, gov.uk says the online return must reach HMRC by 11:59pm on
**31 January 2027**, and the tax owed must be paid by the same time. The paper deadline was 31 October 2026.

## The records to keep

gov.uk asks you to keep records of your business miles for your vehicles. HMRC's manual says it's "important that a
contemporaneous record of business mileage is maintained to support any claim using the flat rate": a record kept as
you go. A log with the date, where each journey started and ended, its business purpose and the miles makes each trip
easy to check. If you claim actual costs, keep the receipts and other proof of what you spent.

**How long:** gov.uk says you "must keep your records for at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of
the relevant tax year". For the 2025 to 2026 return, due by 31 January 2027, that is until at least the end of January
2032.

### Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

gov.uk, read on 17 November 2026, says Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords

over a threshold:

Partnerships will join later, on a timeline HMRC hasn't set yet. Those it applies to keep digital records of their

## What to check with HMRC or an accountant for your own case

This guide explains HMRC's rules and its method. It doesn't say which method suits your business or what you can claim.
A few points depend on your own situation:

- **Which method for each vehicle.** gov.uk's simplified expenses checker compares the two. A choice of the flat rate
  stays with that vehicle for as long as you use it in the business.
- **Whether a journey is business travel**, especially if you work from home, at several places, or combine business
  with something private. gov.uk says: "If you're not sure whether a business cost is an allowable expense contact
  HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC)."
- **Accounts that don't run from 6 April to 5 April, a partnership, or a vehicle you've claimed capital allowances on.**
  HMRC's manual covers each; HMRC or an accountant can apply it to your figures.
- **Making Tax Digital.** gov.uk has a tool to check if and when it applies to you.
